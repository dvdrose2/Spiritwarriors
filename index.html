About the series

Faith that actually costs something.

The Spirit Warriors follows a close-knit group of teenagers in the small town of Lincoln who discover that the power of God is real. It begins with a Bible and a backyard shed, and grows into revival, spiritual warfare, and a future where the pressure to compromise comes with laws, cards and cameras.

Each book blends fast action (car chases, hostage crises, martial arts) with honest questions: Is the Bible reliable? Is Jesus really the only way? What do you do when everyone around you says “just go along”? Scripture is quoted from the King James Version, and the friends learn to use it the way Jesus did: to answer, to test, and to stand.

Cover art from Awakening, Book One.

Action & suspense

Hostages, kidnappings, midnight radio signals, black cars and a fight in the dark: real stakes that keep the pages turning.

The Word of God

Every Bible quotation is in the King James Version, woven into the story through questions, arguments and hard decisions rather than lectures.

Real friendship

Humor, pizza, arguments and apologies. The friends sharpen one another, forgive one another and show up for one another.

Holiness & truth

Genuine conversion shows in repentance, obedience and a changed life, and these books say so plainly and kindly.

Meet the Spirit Warriors

A team you’ll want in your corner.

Six friends, each with strengths, flaws and a story of their own. (Light spoilers from Books One to Three.)

J

Jason Williams

The prayerful leader

A good-humored African American teen from Elm Street who can make friends with anyone. The mysterious Bible lands in Jason’s hands in Book One, and from then on he leads by praying first and speaking boldly. By Book Four he is learning that real leadership grows through serving others. He lives with his mom, Karen, and his little sister, Nia.

  • Prayer
  • Preaching
  • Servant leadership
T

Tyler Bennett

The apologist

The thoughtful one. Tyler loves history and science, plays quarterback, and keeps a backpack full of documentaries and a whiteboard full of arrows. When hard questions come (creation, the Trinity, the cross, Bible manuscripts, strange lights in the sky), he digs in and answers with Scripture, patience and respect, whether he is talking with a Muslim classmate or a sincere Latter-day Saint.

  • Apologetics
  • History
  • Respectful debate
A

Alex Reynolds

Football, Kenpo & comic relief

The class clown with the whoopee cushion and a heart even bigger than his jokes. Alex is a football player whose years of American Kenpo have toned him up and slimmed him down, and who works his way up to a brown belt. His loyalty is fierce. His temper is the battle he has to bring to God.

  • Humor
  • Kenpo
  • Loyalty
  • Self-control
S

Sophie Goldstein

The martial artist

Smart, driven and “tough as nails,” Sophie has trained in her father’s Krav Maga dojo since she was five. She is a Jewish follower of Jesus, a courageous fighter who knows that real strength includes self-control, and the first to count the exits when trouble starts.

  • Krav Maga
  • Courage
  • Sharp mind
M

Mia Kim

The compassionate heart

The heart of the group. Mia brings snacks, checks on everyone, loves animals and loves people even more. She is also the artist who designs the friends’ gospel tracts and fills a sketchbook with the people she loves. Gentle and spiritually sensitive, she is often the first to notice who is hurting.

  • Compassion
  • Art
  • Spiritual sensitivity
L

Layla Nasser

The seeker who became a sister

A brilliant, devout Muslim girl from the family that runs Nasser’s Market, ready to take on every argument Tyler has. Her honest search for the truth costs her family and home, and she finds a new family with the Goldsteins. She carries her grandmother’s red scarf, and a love for her younger brother, Zayd, that never fades.

  • Honest searching
  • Sharp mind
  • Costly faith

The key adults

Avi Goldstein

Mentor & Krav Maga instructor

Sophie’s Israeli-born father and the group’s trainer and mentor. He tells Alex: “Skill without wisdom is just a faster way to get hurt.”

Frank Mazur

Broadcaster & ally

A midnight shortwave-radio host who starts out as a lonely conspiracy theorist, and becomes a believer and a resourceful friend to the families, putting what he once hoarded in fear to use for others.

Mark Reynolds

Alex’s father & former police officer

A sharp, steady investigator who spent years on the police force. His honest struggle between his badge and his conscience shapes the whole family.

Ellen Reynolds

Alex’s mother & nurse

A veteran nurse whose care for the sick doesn’t depend on whose card they carry. With Karen Williams, she helps run the informal clinic in Book Four.

Pastor Dale Whitfield

Church leader

White-haired, soft-spoken and wise, with half-moon reading glasses, Pastor Whitfield shepherds the Main Street congregation. When its doors are padlocked, he keeps the church going in living rooms.

What teens gain

More than a good story.

These books don’t just entertain. They show what faithful, everyday courage looks like, and they send teens back to the Bible to find it for themselves.

Courage

Courage here is quiet as often as it’s loud: speaking up at school, staying kind in a humiliating line, standing when it’s easier to sit.

In the series: the friends keep telling the truth when it costs them school, jobs and reputations.

Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid, neither be thou dismayed: for the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest. (Joshua 1:9)

Standing firm under peer pressure

When everyone says “just go along, it’s easier,” what do you do? The books give teens real scenes to think through before the pressure hits.

In the series: classmates, teammates and old friends push the Spirit Warriors to “be one,” stay quiet and get compliant.

And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind (Romans 12:2)

Apologetics

Teens learn to give a reason for their hope: calmly, respectfully and from Scripture. Tyler models how to listen before you answer.

In the series: conversations on creation, the Trinity, the cross, Bible manuscripts, Islam and Latter-day Saint beliefs.

but sanctify the Lord God in your hearts: and be ready always to give an answer to every man that asketh you a reason of the hope that is in you with meekness and fear (1 Peter 3:15)

Purity & holiness

Real conversion shows in repentance and a changed life. The books treat holiness as the fruit of knowing Jesus, not a rulebook, and show characters wrestling honestly with temper, pride and compromise.

In the series: Alex confronts his anger, Frank lets go of fear, and Mark decides to be “all in.”

Flee also youthful lusts: but follow righteousness, faith, charity, peace, with them that call on the Lord out of a pure heart. (2 Timothy 2:22)

Real friendship

Six friends who pray, argue, eat pizza, apologize and show up. The clubhouse and the kitchen table are as central to the story as any battle.

In the series: a friend group that welcomes the outsider, forgives the hothead and sticks together when it’s dangerous.

Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend. (Proverbs 27:17)

Discernment against deception

A smooth voice. A beautiful message. A friendlier Bible. The series teaches teens to test everything against the Word of God rather than trust a feeling or a crowd.

In the series: strange lights, a “gentler” pamphlet and global broadcasts, all tested verse by verse.

Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world. (1 John 4:1)

For parents & leaders

Fiction you can hand your teen with confidence.

You’re not looking for another distraction. You’re looking for something that builds your teen up. These books are written to start the conversations you hope to have.

Clean content

No swearing in Books Two, Three and Four, and alcohol is never glorified: where drinking appears, it is shown for what it does to people.

Biblically sound

All Scripture is quoted from the King James Version. The books hold evangelical Protestant convictions: salvation by grace through faith in Jesus Christ, genuine conversion that produces repentance and obedience, and the authority of the Bible.

Built for conversation

Books Two, Three and Four each end with a For Discussion section of Scripture-based questions. Book Four alone has eighteen, each tied to a scene and a King James passage.

Conversation starters

  1. People in the series spend years exposing other people’s secrets while hiding their own. Where do we need Jesus to cover us instead of covering things up ourselves?
  2. When is it right to stay and stand firm, and when is it wise to leave? But when they persecute you in this city, flee ye into another (Matthew 10:23)
  3. How can we love someone who disagrees with us without pretending we agree?
  4. What would you do if it became illegal to say that Jesus is the only way?
  5. Why does even a beautiful message still need to be tested against the Bible?
  6. Who has God asked you to forgive?

Home, homeschool & youth group

Read aloud as a family, assign a chapter a night for a homeschool literature and Bible block, or run a youth-group book club using the discussion questions. Books One and Three also include a plain-language guide to students’ religious-liberty rights, such as starting a Bible club at school.

A candid note

These are action-thrillers. Parents of younger or sensitive readers may want to read along, because the stories include occult and demonic scenes (Book One), kidnappings and a hostage crisis (Books Two and Three), and a violent assault (Book Four), always with real consequences and never glamorized.

The series is fiction. Names, organizations and events, including Concord, One Table and the Visitors, are imaginary, and characters’ opinions are their own.

Discussion question sampler

Questions worth talking about.

Six of the eighteen questions from the back of The Underground Church, each paired with the King James verse it explores. (They mention a few details from Book Four.)

1

Jason’s first defeat in the grocery line is small and ordinary. Read Matthew 5:10–12.

Blessed are they which are persecuted for righteousness’ sake: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. (Matthew 5:10)

Why might a quiet humiliation be a harder test than a public showdown?

3

The Visitors sound peaceful, ancient, and kind. Read Galatians 1:8 and 2 Corinthians 11:14.

But though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed. (Galatians 1:8)

Why does a beautiful message still have to be tested against Scripture?

5

The friends go out on Fridays and leave before a patrol arrives. Read Matthew 10:16.

Behold, I send you forth as sheep in the midst of wolves: be ye therefore wise as serpents, and harmless as doves. (Matthew 10:16)

When is leaving wisdom, and when would it be fear wearing a disguise?

6

When the church building is padlocked, the church meets in garages, kitchens, and basements. Read Acts 5:42.

And daily in the temple, and in every house, they ceased not to teach and preach Jesus Christ. (Acts 5:42)

What does the underground church do that a locked door cannot stop?

7

A pamphlet changes Jesus’ words from “the way” to “a way.” Read John 14:6 and Isaiah 40:8.

Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me. (John 14:6)

Why does a changing world always want a softer Bible?

17

Jason believes the church will go through the tribulation, not around it. Read Matthew 24:9–13 and 24:29–31.

But he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved. (Matthew 24:13)

How is that hope different from panic, and different from denial?

Awakening Book One

Five friends at Lincoln High (Jason, Alex, Tyler, Mia and Sophie) have been inseparable since they were small. Then Jason’s world cracks open, and the friends begin meeting in a backyard shed with Bibles, notebooks and hard questions. What they find there sends them back into school, into the woods and into a town where something is deeply wrong.

What’s inside

  • An English class steeped in wizards and spells becomes the first battleground.
  • A secret lodge, a coven in the woods and the question of who in Lincoln is really in charge.
  • Alex’s father, Officer Mark Reynolds, starts digging into dangerous secrets.
  • A classroom debate on creation versus evolution, and a growing revival in the park.
  • The first hints of the end-times themes that grow through the series.
  • A closing guide for students on their religious-liberty rights, including how to start a Bible club.

Themes

The reality of the spiritual world, the authority of Scripture, boldness at school, and the gift of friends who pray together.

Key verse

For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places. (Ephesians 6:12)

The Cover-Up Book Two

The coven is gone, and the church on Main Street is full. Then Jason hands a tract to the wrong man in the park. Frank Mazur runs a midnight shortwave show and believes secret societies run the world. Most people think he’s harmless, but his scanners caught something real the night Mayor Thorne died, and now black cars follow him everywhere.

What’s inside

  • Kidnappings, car chases, a locked warehouse and a fight against five men in the dark.
  • A friendship that grows out of a history project: Tyler and Layla Nasser, who is ready to take on every argument he has.
  • Hard, respectful questions about the Trinity, the cross and the end of the age.
  • A truthful way to talk with someone who loves conspiracy theories: neither mocking him nor agreeing with what can’t be proven.
  • Twelve discussion questions at the back.

Themes

Truth and honesty, the difference between exposing other people’s secrets and having your own sin covered, the cost of following Jesus, and being “all in” instead of half in.

Key verse

For there is nothing covered, that shall not be revealed; neither hid, that shall not be known. (Luke 12:2)

A Time to Run Book Three

Eight months after Christmas Eve, the friends are juniors, Alex is about to earn his brown belt and Layla has found a new home with the Goldsteins. On the last Friday of August, strange lights hang over the reservoir. A movement called One Table promises that Christians will never be divided again. A digital ID called Concord, with an optional chip “for your safety,” is completely voluntary. Everybody says so.

What’s inside

  • A mall locked from the inside, hostages and gunmen, and a fight through the service corridors.
  • Honest conversations about sincerity versus salvation, and why unity without truth isn’t unity.
  • New laws, new pledges and hard decisions for families.
  • A journey into the hills, and the trust it takes to leave home.
  • A “Note on Students’ Rights” and discussion questions at the back.

Themes

Truth versus false unity, standing firm versus fleeing, and the freedom of conscience that belongs to everyone, including people who disagree with us.

Key verse

But when they persecute you in this city, flee ye into another (Matthew 10:23)

The Underground Church Book Four

The families are home in Lincoln, with gray Provisional Resident cards instead of the Concord implant. Lincoln High will not admit them, Pastor Whitfield’s church is padlocked, and believers meet in home groups of nine or fewer. The state calls it all equal and voluntary even as the pressure increases.

What’s inside

  • A society of two classes: a grocery line, a transit stop and a hospital desk that all say “not you.”
  • An unexpected provision for the families, and a turning point for Frank.
  • Short Friday-afternoon outreaches with real risk and real spiritual encounters.
  • The Visitors’ global broadcasts and a gentler “gospel” that Jason and Tyler test against Scripture.
  • A respectful, honest conversation between Tyler and a sincere young Latter-day Saint.
  • A church built from nursing, teaching, feeding and sheltering, and a public gathering that goes terribly wrong.
  • Eighteen King James discussion questions at the back.

Themes

The Church as the people of Jesus rather than a building, obedience to Christ when compromise is easier, mercy over revenge, and prayer together with responsible care.

Key verse

Then Peter and the other apostles answered and said, We ought to obey God rather than men. (Acts 5:29)
